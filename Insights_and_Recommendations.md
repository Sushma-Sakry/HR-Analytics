# Task 7: Insights and Recommendations

## Key Insights

**1. Overall employee attrition**

The cleaned dataset contains 1,473 employee records, of which 237 employees left the company. The overall attrition rate is 16.09%. This indicates that employee retention is an area worth investigating.

**2. Overtime and attrition**

Employees who worked overtime had an attrition rate of 30.53%, compared with 10.41% among employees who did not work overtime. The higher rate among overtime employees suggests that workload and work-life balance should be investigated.

**3. Attrition by department**

The Sales department had the highest department-level attrition rate at 20.58%, followed by Human Resources at 19.05% and Research & Development at 13.81%. Retention challenges may differ across departments.

**4. Attrition by age group**

Employees aged 18–25 had an attrition rate of approximately 35.77% (44 departures among 123 employees), the highest among the age groups examined. Early-career employee retention may deserve additional attention.

**5. Attrition by job role**

Sales Representatives had the highest job-role attrition rate at 39.29% (33 departures among 84 employees). Laboratory Technicians had a rate of 23.85%, while Research Directors had a rate of 2.50%.

## Recommendations

**1. Review overtime workload**

The company should review overtime patterns, workload distribution, staffing levels, and employee feedback, particularly among employees with frequent overtime. It could pilot workload adjustments and monitor whether attrition changes over time.

**2. Develop targeted retention initiatives**

The company should investigate reasons for departures among Sales Representatives and younger employees. Possible actions include structured onboarding, mentoring, career-development opportunities, regular manager check-ins, and employee feedback sessions. Their effectiveness should be evaluated using attrition rates over time.

## Conclusion

The analysis highlights differences in attrition across overtime status, departments, age groups, and job roles. These findings can help HR teams prioritize further investigation and design targeted retention initiatives. The results describe associations in the available dataset and do not establish that any single factor causes employees to leave.
